Ends at one point (LO1)

Two ends at the same point go to different sides: the segment whose end goes up lies higher. The other order lays the two lines on top of each other.

Without the rule: ends at the same position set no order.

Input
{"tree": [["Doc", ["A1", "A2", "A3", ["S", ["S1"]], "D1"]]], "edges": [["A1", "A3"], ["A2", "D1"], ["D1", "A3"], ["S1", "A1"], ["S1", "A3"]]}

Stand-in positions do not coincide (EX1)

A position that stands for a lane not known yet can equal another one while the lanes differ, so it never counts as the same point.

Without the rule: a position that stands for an unknown lane counts as exact.

Reasons by strength (LO2)

Each order between two segments has a reason. The stronger reasons are taken first.

Without the rule: the orders of a channel are not taken by strength.

{"tree": [["Doc", ["A1", ["S", ["S1", "S2"]], "D1", "D2", "D3"]]], "edges": [["D1", "S2"], ["D2", "D3"], ["D2", "S1"], ["S1", "D2"]]}

A weaker order gives way (LO3)

An order that contradicts the stronger orders already taken is dropped.

Without the rule: an order that closes a cycle is not dropped.

Pocket first (LO5)

Under the columns, of two overlapping segments, the one with the higher base height lies higher. A pocket is empty space for every line that fits. A row keeps the height of its lane and has no base height.

Without the rule: the base heights do not order the segments under the columns.

Input
{"tree": [["Doc", ["A1", "A2", "A3", ["S", ["S1", ["SR", ["S2", "S3"]]]], "D1", ["Q", ["Q1", ["QR", ["Q2"]]]], "E1", "E2"]]], "edges": [["D1", "A2"], ["E1", "Q1"], ["E2", "Q1"], ["Q1", "S3"], ["S1", "E2"]]}

Base heights before ends at one point (LO6)

The base heights under the columns are the strongest reason for the order of two segments, stronger than two ends at one point: under the columns the heights, not a choice, decide which segment lies higher.

Right-hand traffic (TR1)

Two overlapping segments of different directions whose ends set no order: the one that goes left lies above, the one that goes up lies right.

Without the rule: right-hand traffic is mirrored.

Input
{"tree": [["Doc", ["A1", ["S", ["S1", "S2"]], "C1", "C2"]]], "edges": [["C1", "S2"], ["C2", "S1"], ["C2", "S2"]]}

Exit priority (R2)

An unavoidable crossing of a segment nested in another one lies near the source.

Without the rule: lane priority inverted.

Shared stretch (ST1)

Two routes that pass the same chain of channels keep one order on the whole chain.

Without the rule: shared stretches of two or more channels are not ordered.

Stretch only where the segments overlap (ST3)

A channel at the end of a stretch where the segments only touch does not belong to the stretch.

Without the rule: a shared stretch keeps the channels where the routes only touch.

A stretch of one channel (ST4)

A single common channel is a stretch too.

Without the rule: a single common channel is not a stretch.

A stretch trimmed to one channel (ST5)

No stretch inside a stretch (ST8)

A single channel inside a longer stretch is no stretch of its own: it could order the pair against the whole stretch.

Without the rule: a single channel inside a longer stretch is a stretch of its own.

A through pass is no turn (ST6)

A route goes on straight through a through pass and leaves the stretch where it really turns.

Without the rule: a through pass counts as the place where a route turns off.

Turns to different sides (ST7)

At an end of a stretch where the routes turn off to different sides, each route lies on the side it turns to.

Without the rule: an end where the routes turn off to different sides sets no order.

A pair between shared faces (ST9)

A pair whose stretch ends on shared faces at both ends keeps the order of right-hand traffic on the whole stretch.

Without the rule: a pair between shared faces gets no order.

A pair that must cross (ST10)

Two routes that go the same way and must cross keep the order of the end where they come together, and cross where they part.

Without the rule: a pair whose ends require different orders gets no order.

Lanes of a column channel (CL1)

Lanes = max(segments to the left, segments to the right) + segments across. Segments to the left and to the right share lanes, whatever their directions.
